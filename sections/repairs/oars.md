## Scope and escalation

This page covers common in‑house oar repairs:

- Spoon delamination **stabilisation** (small areas)
- Sleeve replacement and pitch alignment
- Fixed handle replacement (legacy)

> [!WARNING] Stop-use
> Do not use an oar if the shaft is cracked, the blade‑to‑shaft joint is damaged, or there is delamination that can be flexed by hand.

Escalate to manufacturer/pro repair when:

- The delamination is large, near the blade‑to‑shaft joint, or repeats.
- The shaft is damaged (crack/soft spot).
- You cannot guarantee pitch alignment / matching between a pair.

## Spoon delamination stabilisation

Goal: stop water ingress and prevent a small delamination from growing. This is not the same as a full structural rebuild.

[!PROCEDURE:Intermediate] Stabilise a small spoon delamination (inject + clamp)
Stabilise a small delamination on the spoon by injecting low‑viscosity epoxy and clamping flat.

> [!WARNING] Important
> If the delamination is extensive, near the blade‑to‑shaft joint, or you can flex it significantly, escalate.

**Tools:** Low‑viscosity epoxy, syringe, tape, release film, flat clamping pads, clamps/weights, drill + tiny bit (optional)

**Steps:**

1. Clean and dry the area thoroughly.
2. Define the delam boundary by tap test; mark with tape.
3. If needed, drill small injection/vent holes at the edge of the pocket (1–2 mm).
4. Inject epoxy slowly until it wets out (epoxy appears at vent or along the boundary).
5. Cover with release film and clamp with broad pads to restore the original flat/curve.
6. Hold until full cure; then remove clamp and clean excess.
7. Seal the injection holes and sand/finish as needed.

**Check:** Delam no longer flexes; tap test returns closer to “sharp”; no open path for water ingress.
[!/PROCEDURE]

### When to send to manufacturer

Send to the manufacturer or a specialist repairer if:

- Delam is near the blade‑to‑shaft joint.
- Delam continues to grow after stabilisation.
- The spoon has a crack through the skin.
- The oar is an important race pair and matching must be preserved exactly.

## Sleeve replacement and pitch alignment

Sleeve replacement is a **precision** operation: a small pitch error is obvious on the water.

> [!INFO] Mark everything
> Before removing anything, mark: collar position, sleeve position, and a pitch reference line.

[!PROCEDURE:Advanced] Replace an oar sleeve and restore pitch
Remove a damaged sleeve, bond a new sleeve, and restore pitch and inboard to match a reference oar.

**Tools:** Replacement sleeve, epoxy, heat gun (controlled), scraper, sandpaper, pitch reference/jig, marker, calipers/tape, clamps/fixture, PPE

**Steps:**

1. **Reference and record:**
   - Measure and record collar position (inboard).
   - Record sleeve-to-spoon distance and any alignment marks.
   - Photograph P/S marks and blade back face relationship.
2. **Remove old sleeve:**
   - Apply gentle heat to soften epoxy (avoid overheating carbon).
   - Cut/peel sleeve off carefully; avoid gouging the shaft.
3. **Surface prep:**
   - Remove old adhesive fully.
   - Sand the bonding area to clean, matte surface; wipe with IPA.
4. **Dry fit:**
   - Slide new sleeve on without epoxy; confirm position and rotation.
   - Use a known‑good reference oar or pitch jig to set rotation.
   - Draw alignment marks across sleeve/shaft so you can re‑set it during bonding.
5. **Bond:**
   - Mix epoxy accurately.
   - Apply a thin, even layer; slide sleeve into place; align to marks.
   - Remove squeeze‑out; fit collar/clams as required (if applicable).
6. **Hold alignment for cure:**
   - Use a fixture or clamp blocks so sleeve cannot rotate.
   - Maintain cure temperature per epoxy spec.
7. **Verify:**
   - After full cure, verify pitch on the boat or with a pitch gauge.
   - Verify inboard matches the reference set.

**Check:** Pitch matches reference; collar position correct; sleeve is fully bonded with no gaps.
[!/PROCEDURE]

## Fixed handle replacement (legacy)

Fixed handles are uncommon in modern fleets but still appear on older sweep oars.

[!PROCEDURE:Intermediate] Replace a fixed handle (legacy)
Replace a fixed handle on an older oar, ensuring correct alignment and secure bonding.

**Tools:** Replacement handle, epoxy, heat gun, sandpaper, alignment marks, PPE

**Steps:**

1. Measure and record handle orientation and any indexing marks.
2. Remove handle with controlled heat; clean adhesive.
3. Prep surfaces (sand + IPA).
4. Bond new handle aligned to marks; hold until full cure.
5. Verify no rotation under load.

**Check:** Handle secure; orientation correct.
[!/PROCEDURE]
