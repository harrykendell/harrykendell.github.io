# Footplates

![Heel restraints are safety-critical. In any boat with fitted shoes, each shoe must be independently restrained and the heel must not rise more than 7 cm.](imgs/overviews/footplate_heel_restraint.svg){size=0.5 float=right}

Different boats use different stretcher and steering systems (wing‑nut tracks, quick‑release clamps, wire rope, Dyneema, rigid pushrods). Always confirm the exact routing and parts on that boat’s rigging sheet.

> [!INFO] Photograph before disassembly
> Footplate and steering routing varies by boat. A quick “before” photo prevents 80% of reassembly mistakes.

## Footplate adjustment and inspection

[!PROCEDURE:Beginner] Footplate set-up (athlete adjustment)
Quick pre‑rowing adjustment of stretcher position to the athlete's preferred setting.

**Tools:** None (or wing nuts / quick‑release clamp depending on system)

**Steps:**

1. Set stretcher position to the athlete's number/mark.
2. Confirm left/right are symmetric (count holes/marks).
3. Confirm all clamps/nuts are tight.
4. Push/pull firmly on the footplate: there should be no rocking.

**Check:** No wobble; symmetric placement. Do before every outing.
[!/PROCEDURE]

[!PROCEDURE:Intermediate] Footplate fastener scan (structural)
Weekly/biweekly check that footplate mounts and tracks are secure and that inserts are not starting to spin.

> [!WARNING] Important
> Over‑tightening strips inserts. Tighten only until secure; if an insert spins or pulls, stop and escalate.

**Tools:** Correct spanners/Allen keys, spare washers/nuts, marker

**Steps:**

1. Check the footplate track/rail fasteners: tight, correct washers present, no corrosion.
2. Check the stretcher adjustment mechanism (wing nuts/clamps): no cracked parts; threads not stripped.
3. Check shoe mounts and heel restraint anchors: secure and not cracked.
4. Add witness marks to fasteners that frequently loosen (if your fleet uses witness marks).

**Check:** No movement at mounts; no spinning inserts; adjustment mechanism works smoothly.
[!/PROCEDURE]

## Heel restraints (safety-critical)

> [!WARNING] Heel restraints and foot release are safety-critical
> In any boat with fitted shoes, each shoe must be independently restrained and the **heel must not be able to rise more than 7 cm** (measured at right angles from the footplate). The foot release must be **self‑acting** (should not require intervention by athlete or rescuer).
>
> Do not use **laces** or **cable ties** as heel restraints; use durable cord/strap designed for the job.
>
> **References:**
>
> - [British Rowing heel restraint guidance](https://www.britishrowing.org/2016/04/do-you-check-your-heel-restraints/)
> - [British Rowing Rules of Racing (current edition)](https://www.britishrowing.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/2025-British-Rowing-Rules-of-Racing.pdf)
> - [British Rowing safety alert (heel restraints)](https://www.britishrowing.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/Safety-Alert-Heel-Restraints.pdf)

[!PROCEDURE:Beginner] Heel restraint inspection and replacement
Safety‑critical check and replacement of heel ties to ensure foot retention in case of capsize.

> [!WARNING] Stop-use
> If any heel restraint is broken or missing, stop‑use until corrected.

**Tools:** Suitable cord/strap, scissors, lighter/heat to seal ends

**Steps:**

1. Inspect both heel ties for fraying, looseness, broken knots, or missing anchors.
2. Measure heel lift: the heel must not rise more than **7 cm** from the footplate.
3. Replace if worn/broken:
   - Remove old tie completely.
   - Thread new cord/strap through the shoe anchor and the footplate anchor (boat‑dependent).
   - Tie securely with a knot that cannot slip (e.g., double fisherman's / figure‑eight follow‑through) and trim ends.
   - Heat‑seal synthetic ends to prevent fraying (do not burn the shoe).
4. Confirm the shoe releases freely without intervention (self‑acting).
5. Repeat for the other shoe (independent restraint).

**Check:** Both shoes restrained; heel lift ≤ 7 cm; release is self‑acting.

> [!INFO] Frequency
> Check before every outing. Replace immediately if worn.
[!/PROCEDURE]

## Steering wire

[!PROCEDURE:Beginner] Steering functional check
Quick pre‑outing check that steering is centred and moves freely. If steering binds, goes slack, or will not centre, do not launch until fixed.

**Tools:** None

**Steps:**

1. With the boat supported, centre the toe steering and confirm the rudder is centred.
2. Move toes to full travel left/right; confirm smooth, even rudder travel with no snags.
3. Check cable/rope tension throughout travel (no slack loops, no rubbing on sharp edges).

**Check:** Rudder centred at toe‑centre; full travel smooth and repeatable. Do before every outing.
[!/PROCEDURE]

[!PROCEDURE:Intermediate] Replace steering cable (wire/rope)
Replace a frayed or snapped steering cable to restore full steering responsiveness.

**Tools:** Replacement cable/rope, cutters, clamps/stops, crimping tool (if using ferrules), tape/heatshrink

**Steps:**

1. Photograph routing and attachment points (toe mechanism and rudder yoke).
2. Remove the old cable; if possible, use it to pull the new cable through the same routing.
3. Attach at the toe mechanism first.
4. Centre toes and centre rudder.
5. Attach at the rudder yoke:
   - Set equal tension left/right.
   - Verify full travel without the cable going slack.
6. Secure and finish cable ends (ferrules + tape/heatshrink) so there are no sharp strands.
7. Wet‑proof any exposed cable ends where water sits (boat‑dependent).

**Check:** Rudder centred when toes centred; smooth travel; no snagging or slack. Replace when frayed/snapped or if centring repeatedly drifts.
[!/PROCEDURE]

> [!WARNING] Stop-use criteria (footplate/steering)
>
> - Broken or missing heel restraint.
> - Steering cable frayed or rudder linkage loose.
> - Footplate mount loose or cracked.
