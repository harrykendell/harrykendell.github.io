This guide is for repainting  club blades, as a complete process this will take more than a day to complete as the paint must be allowed to dry between steps, you should should ensure captains are aware the blades are being painted. 

The club blades should be painted: **A club green (#00491E) blade with a centred white diagonal stripe on both sides. The stripe should be a uniform thickness when viewed from the front, extending from the loom to the bottom corner.**

The guide is based on the [Riverside blade-painting website](https://www.riversideboatclub.com/blade-painting), but adapted for our paint, materials, and design. It assumes no previous painting experience: do the steps in order, do not rush drying, and ask for help if needed.

> [!DANGER] 2K aerosol safety
> We use **2K ADC aerosol paint**, not ordinary craft spray paint. It is therefore critical that you take proper safety precautions.
>
> Spray only in a controlled, well-ventilated  area away from other people or ignition sources and always read the [Safety Data Sheet](https://www.yourspraypaints.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/sds-sc601-2k-adc-safety-data-sheet-your-spray-paints.pdf). Wear gloves, eye protection, and a mask  suitable for paint aerosols. If you cannot meet those conditions, do not spray.

> [!WARNING] Do not paint damaged blades
> Do not paint over cracks, soft spots, or spoon delamination. Paint hides damage; it does not repair it, report the damage so it may be fixed first.

## Finish standard

The finished blade should look clean and consistent across the whole set, they represent the club, so we should make an effort to present ourselves positively:

- The **main colour is club green** on both faces and around the blade edges.
- There is **one white diagonal stripe** across the centre of each blade.
- The stripe is straight, the same consistent width on every blade.
- The shaft, sleeve, collar, grip, and if present Vortex edge are not painted.
- There should be no runs, dry dusty patches, fingerprints, tape tears, or loose edges.

## Materials
- 2k ADC aerosol (Traffic white + Pearl Green)
- 240 grit wet-and-dry sandpaper
- Isopropyl Alcohol
- Masking/painters tape
- Blue cloth or cloths
- Mask and eye protection

> [!INFO] No primer
> For this paint system we do **not** add primer. Our 2K ADC aerosol paint is used directly on a correctly sanded and cleaned blade. Sanding and cleaning are not optional: the paint needs a dull, keyed surface to adhere to.

## Procedures

[!PROCEDURE:Beginner] Prepare the blade for paint
Prepare the blade surface so the new paint can key properly.

**Tools:** 240 grit wet-and-dry sandpaper, IPA spray bottle, clean cloths, masking tape, gloves, eye protection

**Steps:**

1. Wash off mud, salt, grease, and loose dirt. Let the blade dry.
2. Inspect both faces, the edges, the blade-to-shaft joint, and the sleeve area.
3. Mask anything that must not be painted:
   - shaft
   - sleeve
   - collar
   - grip
   - separate Vortex/wear edge if fitted
4. Sand the paintable blade surface by hand with **240 grit wet-and-dry** until it is evenly dull.
5. Sand edges lightly so the new paint can wrap around the edge, but do not flatten or reshape the spoon.
6. Do not sand through to fibres. If fibres appear, stop and escalate.
7. Spray IPA onto a clean cloth, then wipe the blade to remove dust and grease.
8. Let the IPA evaporate fully before painting.

**Check:** Surface is clean, dry, dull, and dust-free. No glossy islands. No exposed fibres. Masking protects all non-painted parts.
[!/PROCEDURE]

[!PROCEDURE:Intermediate] Spray the white stripe base
Create a white base area wide enough to sit under the final diagonal stripe.

**Tools:** White 2K ADC aerosol, PPE for spraying, cardboard/drop sheet, blade supports

**Steps:**

1. Set the blade flat on supports over cardboard or a drop sheet.
2. Confirm the area is clear of other people and ignition sources.
3. Put on spraying PPE before shaking or spraying the can.
4. Shake the can exactly as directed on the label.
5. Spray a **broad diagonal white band** where the final stripe will go. Make it wider than the final stripe so the stripe edges will be clean after masking.
6. Use several light passes rather than one wet coat.
7. Follow the can label for flash time between coats.
8. Paint both faces and the blade edge where the stripe crosses the edge.
9. Leave the blade flat until the white paint is dry enough to handle without marking.

**Check:** White band covers the future stripe area on both faces. No runs. No fingerprints. Edges are covered where the stripe crosses them.
[!/PROCEDURE]

[!PROCEDURE:Beginner] Mask the final white diagonal stripe
Protect the white stripe so the rest of the blade can be sprayed green.

**Tools:** Masking / painter's tape, pencil, ruler or straight edge, clean dry cloth

**Steps:**

1. Wait until the white paint is dry enough to tape without lifting or denting. If unsure, leave it longer.
2. Place the blade face-up with the shaft pointing right.
3. Mark the stripe position lightly. Keep it centred and diagonal according to the layout convention above.
4. Apply one continuous strip of tape over the stripe if the tape is the desired stripe width.
5. If the tape is too narrow, use two straight tape edges and fill the protected stripe area with additional tape.
6. Press the tape edge down firmly with a clean gloved finger or cloth. Do not use a dirty thumb.
7. Wrap the tape cleanly around the blade edge so the stripe continues over the edge.
8. Flip the blade and repeat on the second face.
9. Lay the set together and compare stripe direction, width, and position before spraying green.

**Check:** The white stripe area is fully protected. Tape edges are straight and sealed. Every blade in the set matches.
[!/PROCEDURE]

[!PROCEDURE:Intermediate] Spray the green main colour
Paint the exposed blade surface green while preserving the masked white stripe.

**Tools:** Green 2K ADC aerosol, PPE for spraying, cardboard/drop sheet, blade supports

**Steps:**

1. Set the masked blade flat on supports.
2. Confirm the area is still clear, ventilated, and free from ignition sources.
3. Put on spraying PPE before handling the aerosol.
4. Shake the can exactly as directed on the label.
5. Spray the exposed blade surface green using light, even passes.
6. Start each pass off the blade, move across steadily, and release after passing the far edge.
7. Keep the can moving. Do not try to achieve full coverage in one coat.
8. Follow the can label for flash time and apply further light coats until coverage is even.
9. Spray both faces and the exposed blade edges.
10. When the final green coat is dry enough not to run but before it has fully hardened, remove the stripe masking slowly by pulling tape back on itself at a low angle.
11. Leave the blade flat and undisturbed.

**Check:** Green coverage is even. White stripe edge is sharp. No tape adhesive remains. No runs, heavy edges, or dry dusty areas.
[!/PROCEDURE]

[!PROCEDURE:Beginner] Cure, inspect, and return to service
Let the coating harden properly before the oars are used.

**Tools:** Clean supports, inspection light, repair log/photo record

**Steps:**

1. Keep the blades flat while curing. Do not cure them standing vertically with blades pointing upward.
2. Keep fresh paint away from dust, rain, direct handling, and boat bay traffic.
3. Do not stack blades against each other while the coating is soft.
4. Use the paint supplier's drying times and the can label as the minimum guide. For 2K ADC, plan for several days before hard service.
5. Inspect both faces, all edges, and the stripe line.
6. Photograph the finished set and record what was painted and when.
7. Return the oars only when the coating is hard enough that normal handling will not mark it.

**Check:** Paint is hard, clean, and matched across the set. No tackiness, tape lift, fingerprints, or exposed sanded areas.
[!/PROCEDURE]

## Common faults and fixes

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Paint peels when tape is removed | Surface was glossy/dirty, tape applied too early, or tape pulled aggressively | Let cure, sand the failed edge smooth, clean with IPA, and repaint light coats. |
| Runs or sagging | Coat was too heavy or blade was not flat | Let harden fully, sand smooth, clean, and repaint light coats. |
| Fuzzy stripe edge | Tape edge not pressed down or paint applied too heavily at the edge | Let cure, mask just outside the fault, lightly sand, and repaint. |
| Dry dusty finish | Sprayed too far away, too fast, or in wind | Let cure, lightly sand dull, clean, and apply a controlled light coat. |
| Fingerprints | Handled before dry enough | Let cure, sand flat, clean, and repaint. |


> [!WARNING] Do not keep correcting wet paint
> Repeatedly touching or re-spraying a wet fault usually makes it worse. Let the paint harden, then sand and repair the defect deliberately.

## Clean-up and storage

- Clear overspray waste and used masking tape after the final coat is safe to leave.
- Keep aerosols capped, clean the nozzle as directed by the can label, and store aerosols according to the product label/SDS.
- Return IPA and masking/painter's tape to the fuel cabinet.
- Dispose of empty or part-used aerosol cans as hazardous aerosol waste. Do not pierce, crush, or flatten aerosol cans.
