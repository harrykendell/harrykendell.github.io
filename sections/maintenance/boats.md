## Routine care & cleaning

[!PROCEDURE:Beginner] Pre‑outing safety check (shell)
Quick pre‑launch check of safety‑critical items. This is the minimum check if you are taking a boat out of the racks.

**Tools:** None

**Steps:**

1. **Hull/deck:** obvious cracks, deep scrapes, exposed fibres; any sharp edges or protrusions.
2. **Buoyancy:** hatch covers present and seated; bungs fitted (where used). No water sloshing.
3. **Steering & fin:** rudder moves freely; fin secure (no wobble).
4. **Riggers/pins:** nothing loose; no rigger movement at hull; gates latch (see **[Gates](/#maintenance/gates)**).
5. **Footplate:** clamps tight; heel restraints present (see **[Footplates](/#maintenance/footplates)**).
6. **Seats:** seat rolls smoothly end‑to‑end (see **[Seats](/#maintenance/seats)**).

**Check:** If anything is loose, cracked, or missing, stop‑use and escalate.
[!/PROCEDURE]

[!PROCEDURE:Beginner] Post‑outing wash‑down
Routine rinse and wash to remove salt, algae, and grit after every outing. This prevents corrosion, mould, and deterioration of seals.

**Tools:** Hose, bucket, mild soap, microfibre cloths

**Steps:**

1. Rinse hull, riggers, and runners/tracks with fresh water.
2. Wash with mild soap (inside and outside), then rinse thoroughly.
3. Wipe dry with microfibre cloth (especially runners and inside cockpit).
4. Open bow/stern hatches briefly to ventilate. Close once fully dry.
5. Quick visual check: fin/rudder, obvious scratches, loose hardware.

> [!INFO] Check for damage
> Report and tag the boat if you see cracks, soft spots, exposed fibres, or any water ingress.

**Check:** No standing water; tracks feel smooth; no new obvious cracks.
[!/PROCEDURE]

[!PROCEDURE:Intermediate] Maintenance day deep‑clean + fastener scan
Weekly or biweekly deep clean with hardware inspection and targeted disassembly to prevent corrosion and catch loose fasteners early.

> [!WARNING] Safety
> Wear gloves/eye protection for cleaners; avoid aggressive solvents on paint. Do not oil anything on a boat: it attracts grit.

**Tools:** Standard tool kit, brushes, microfibres, diluted vinegar (limescale), replacement washers, nyloc nuts as needed

**Steps:**

1. Remove seats and clean wheels/tracks (see **[Seats](/#maintenance/seats)**).
2. Wipe down riggers, pins, and footplate hardware. Look for corrosion and missing washers.
3. Check all **safety‑critical** fasteners: rigger bolts, pin top nuts/retainers, stretcher mounts, steering linkages.
4. Remove limescale with diluted vinegar where needed; rinse thoroughly.
5. Re‑fit everything and do a quick functional check: seat, steering, gates, footplate clamps.

**Check:** No missing washers; all critical bolts tight; steering functional.

> [!WARNING] Escalation
> Stripped inserts, seized bolts that risk tearing out, or any structural cracking → tag out and escalate.
[!/PROCEDURE]

[!PROCEDURE:Intermediate] Periodic polish (fleet finish)
Machine polish the hull at end‑of‑season to restore finish and protect against staining/UV.

> [!WARNING] Safety
> Eye protection required; keep cables clear of water; do not overheat paint.

**Tools:** Fine‑cut compound, foam pad, low‑RPM polisher, microfibres, wax/sealant (if used)

**Steps:**

1. Wash and dry the hull completely.
2. Machine polish at low RPM, keeping the pad moving.
3. Wipe residue and inspect. Repeat lightly if needed.
4. Apply wax/sealant if part of club policy.

**Check:** Finish restored without burn‑through. If paint is thin or you see primer/carbon, stop and plan professional refinish.
[!/PROCEDURE]

## Storage and transport

**Storage rules (always):**

- Store indoors where possible; avoid long‑term UV exposure.
- Support boats on correct slings/trestles at approved points.
- Keep hatches closed during storage (after drying) to prevent pests and humidity cycling.
- Do not store with riggers bearing abnormal loads; avoid leaning shells against hard edges.

[!PROCEDURE:Beginner] Trailer loading and transport
Safe loading onto a trailer to prevent damage during transport. Proper support and strapping prevents hull flexing and protects critical components.

**Tools:** Straps, padding, fin/rudder protectors, tape

**Steps:**

1. Confirm fin/rudder clearance and protection.
2. Place hull in cradles; ensure contact points are padded.
3. Strap snugly (no deck crushing). Use bow/stern lines to prevent fore‑aft movement.
4. Secure loose riggers or remove if required by trailer geometry.
5. After 10–15 minutes driving, stop and re‑check strap tension.

**Check:** No movement; fin/rudder protected.

> [!WARNING] Important
> If the boat flexes under straps, stop and re‑rig the support points. Large boats require supervision.
[!/PROCEDURE]

## Buoyancy compartments, hatch covers, and bungs (safety-critical)

Water ingress is the hidden killer: it turns minor damage into major repairs and can make a boat unsafe in a capsize.

[!PROCEDURE:Beginner] Buoyancy and hatch check
Safety‑critical check that buoyancy compartments are watertight and that hatch covers and bungs are present and functional.

**Tools:** Towel, flashlight

**Steps:**

1. Confirm bow/stern hatch covers are present, seated fully, and not cracked.
2. Check seals/O‑rings: clean, not split, and seated correctly.
3. Confirm any bungs/vent plugs are present and tightened appropriately.
4. Shine a light inside the compartment: look for standing water, mould, or salt residue.
5. If water is present: drain/dry and **find the leak** (hatch seal, fin box, impact damage).

**Check:** Covers secure; seals intact; compartments dry.

> [!WARNING] Stop‑use criteria (buoyancy)
>
> - Missing hatch cover, missing bung, or a compartment that will not seal.
> - Water sloshing in a sealed compartment (unknown leak).
[!/PROCEDURE]

## Riggers, stays, and structural attachments

Riggers, pins, and stays are the primary load path from athlete → boat. Failures here are safety‑critical and often cascade into hull damage.

[!PROCEDURE:Intermediate] Rigger and stay inspection
Monthly inspection of riggers, pins, and stays to catch movement, cracking, or corrosion before failure. Also do after transport or any impact/bridge strike.

**Tools:** Spanners/Allen keys, spare washers/nuts, marker, straight edge

**Steps:**

1. Check each rigger for visible bends, cracks, or chipped paint exposing aluminium.
2. Try to rock the rigger at the hull interface: there should be **no movement**.
3. Inspect stay wires (where fitted): fraying, broken strands, kinks, loose swages.
4. Confirm washer stacks are present and identical left/right where expected.
5. Re‑tighten fasteners as required (snug; do not crush the hull). Replace nylocs that spin freely.
6. Mark witness lines on key nuts/bolts so loosening is obvious.

**Check:** No movement at mounts; no cracking; stays taut and undamaged.

> [!WARNING] Escalation
> Any crack, bend, or movement → stop‑use; do not “row it gently”.
[!/PROCEDURE]

## Fin, rudder, and fin box

Fins/rudders are frequently damaged during launching, shallow water, and transport. Fin box damage is high‑risk for water ingress.

[!PROCEDURE:Beginner] Fin + rudder quick check
Pre‑outing check that the fin and rudder are secure and aligned. Do this before every outing in steered boats.

**Tools:** None

**Steps:**

1. Confirm fin is fully seated and set screws (if used) are tight.
2. Confirm rudder is securely attached and moves smoothly through full travel.
3. Centre the rudder and confirm alignment with the fin.
4. Check for play: fin/rudder should not wobble relative to the hull.

**Check:** Secure; aligned; smooth travel.

> [!WARNING] Stop‑use criteria
> Looseness, binding, or new cracks around fin box: stop‑use and escalate.
[!/PROCEDURE]

[!PROCEDURE:Intermediate] Fin box leak check (after damage)
After a fin strike or when unexplained water appears in buoyancy compartments, check the fin box for structural damage and cracks that could cause water ingress.

**Tools:** Towel, flashlight, marker

**Steps:**

1. Dry everything thoroughly (inside and outside).
2. Inspect around the fin box: outside cracks, inside staining, lifted laminate (if access exists).
3. Mark any suspect cracks and photograph with a scale.
4. Tag out the boat until the leak path is sealed and assessed.

**Check:** Any damage found should be escalated to professional repair.

> [!INFO] Note
> Fin box damage is a common cause of repeat leaks.
[!/PROCEDURE]

## Inspection and damage triage

> [!WARNING] Stop-use criteria (shell)
> Take the boat **out of service immediately** if any of the following are true:
>
> - Cracks radiating from rigger mounting areas or along structural ribs.
> - Any **soft spot** that is growing, especially on the hull near the waterline or near the fin box.
> - Fin box movement, cracking, or leaking (water ingress).
> - Seat track mounts/inserts moving or pulling out.
> - Delamination you can flex by hand, or that sounds “dead” in a tap test over a large area.
> - Any puncture where core is exposed and has been wet.

[!PROCEDURE:Intermediate] Damage triage (cosmetic vs structural)
Assess damage after any impact or when new damage is observed to decide if it is cosmetic or structural.

**Tools:** Bright light, marker/tape, camera, coin/tap hammer, ruler

**Safety:** Gloves if fibres exposed

**Steps:**

1. Clean the area; damage is hard to classify when dirty.
2. Photograph with a scale (ruler/coin). Mark the boundary with tape.
3. Tap test outward from the damage: listen for change from “sharp” to “dull”.
4. Check both sides (inside cockpit and outside hull) if accessible.
5. Classify:
   - **Cosmetic:** paint/gelcoat scratch with no fibre break, no soft spot.
   - **Minor structural:** shallow fibre damage limited area, no core crush.
   - **Structural:** core crush, delamination, cracks through skin, puncture.

6. Decide next action: seal‑and‑monitor / in‑house repair / professional repair.
7. Log: date, location, photos, who assessed, and whether the boat is restricted/tagged.

**Check:** Damage logged; boat tagged appropriately.

> [!WARNING] Escalation
> Uncertain classification → treat as structural and escalate.
[!/PROCEDURE]
